Detailed review of Dimensions, Kinematics, Forces, Energy Conservation, and Rotational Dynamics.

LECTURE 1

Units, Dimensional Analysis & 1D Kinematics

1. Base Quantities & Dimensional Homogeneity

Physical quantities are classified into Base Quantities (Mass $[M]$, Length $[L]$, Time $[T]$) and Derived Quantities (Speed $[L T^{-1}]$, Acceleration $[L T^{-2}]$, Force $[M L T^{-2}]$, Energy $[M L^2 T^{-2}]$).

Dimensional Homogeneity Rule: Any physical equation is valid ONLY if both sides have identical base dimensions. Terms can only be added or subtracted if they share identical units.

2. Calculus-Based & Uniform 1D Kinematics

Instantaneous velocity $v_x = \frac{dx}{dt}$ and acceleration $a_x = \frac{dv_x}{dt} = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$.

For Constant Acceleration ($a_x = \text{const}$):

  • $v_x = v_{0x} + a_x t$
  • $x - x_0 = v_{0x} t + \frac{1}{2} a_x t^2$
  • $v_x^2 = v_{0x}^2 + 2 a_x (x - x_0)$
  • Free Fall: $a_y = -g = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2$
LECTURE 2

Friction, Mechanical Work & Work-Energy Theorem

1. Friction Laws & Contact Forces

Contact forces split into Normal Force ($N$) perpendicular to contact, and Friction ($f$) parallel to contact. Static friction prevents sliding ($f_s \le \mu_s N$), reaching maximum $(f_s)_{max} = \mu_s N$. Kinetic sliding friction is constant: $f_k = \mu_k N$.

2. Dot Product Work & Kinetic Energy Theorem

Work $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d} = F d \cos\phi$. Spring work (Hooke's Law $F = -kx$): $W_s = \frac{1}{2} k x_1^2 - \frac{1}{2} k x_2^2$.

Work-Energy Theorem: $W_{total} = \Delta K = \frac{1}{2} m v_f^2 - \frac{1}{2} m v_i^2$.

LECTURE 3

Potential Energy & Conservation of Mechanical Energy

1. Conservative vs Non-Conservative Forces

Conservative Forces: Work is path-independent and equals zero over closed loops ($\oint \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{s} = 0$). Work directly equals potential energy drop: $W_{cons} = -\Delta U$.

Non-Conservative Forces: Work depends on path (e.g., Friction) and converts mechanical energy into internal thermal energy ($\Delta U_{int} = f_k d$).

2. Mechanical Energy Equations

Total Mechanical Energy $E = K + U$.

  • Conservative System: $K_1 + U_1 = K_2 + U_2$
  • System with Friction & External Work: $K_1 + U_1 + W_{other} = K_2 + U_2$
  • Gravitational Potential: $U_g = mgy$
LECTURE 4

Rotational Motion, Torque & Moment of Inertia

1. Linear to Rotational Relationships

Angular position $\theta$, velocity $\omega = \frac{d\theta}{dt}$, acceleration $\alpha = \frac{d\omega}{dt}$. Linear equivalents: arc distance $s = r\theta$, tangential speed $v = r\omega$, tangential acceleration $a_t = r\alpha$, centripetal acceleration $a_r = \omega^2 r$. Total acceleration $a = \sqrt{a_t^2 + a_r^2}$.

2. Torque, Moment of Inertia & Energy

Torque $\tau = r F \sin\phi$. Rotational Newton's 2nd Law: $\sum \tau = I \alpha$.

Moment of Inertia $I = \sum m_i r_i^2$. Parallel-Axis Theorem: $I = I_{CM} + M h^2$. Rotational Kinetic Energy $K_{rot} = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$. Rotational Power $P = \tau \omega$.

Extra Lecture Points (from the slides)

  • Power (Lec 3): $P_{av} = \frac{\Delta W}{\Delta t}$, $P = \vec{F}\cdot\vec{v}$, $1\text{ hp} = 746\text{ W}$.
  • Gravity work: $W_{grav} = -\Delta U_{grav} = mg(y_1-y_2)$ (positive going down).
  • Other forces: $W_{other}>0 \Rightarrow E\uparrow$, $W_{other}<0 \Rightarrow E\downarrow$, $0 \Rightarrow E$ constant.
  • Trap: the launch angle never enters an energy-conservation speed calculation (only $|v|$ and height).
  • Constant $\alpha$: $\omega=\omega_0+\alpha t$, $\theta=\omega_0 t+\tfrac12\alpha t^2$, $\omega^2=\omega_0^2+2\alpha\theta$.
  • Point on rim: $a=r\sqrt{\alpha^2+\omega^4}$ (always use radians).
  • $I$ table: rod center $\frac{1}{12}ML^2$, rod end $\frac13 ML^2$, solid cylinder $\frac12 MR^2$, hollow cylinder $MR^2$, solid sphere $\frac25 MR^2$, thin shell $\frac23 MR^2$.
  • Torque is N·m but NOT joule; $1\text{ rpm}=\frac{2\pi}{60}\text{ rad/s}$.

Power: F·v

Master Definitions & Physical Standards Sheet

Complete glossary of core terms, base dimensions $[M L T]$, and SI units across Lectures 1-4.

Base Quantity

Lec 1

A physical quantity defined by direct experimental measurement standard (e.g., Mass $[M]$, Length $[L]$, Time $[T]$).

SI Standard: kg, m, s Dim: $[M], [L], [T]$

Dimensional Homogeneity

Lec 1

The physical requirement that both sides of any valid physical equation must possess identical base dimensions.

Rule: $[LHS] = [RHS]$ Additive Consistency

Kinetic Friction ($f_k$)

Lec 2

Resistive contact force acting parallel to surfaces opposing active sliding motion: $f_k = \mu_k N$.

SI Unit: N (Newton) Dim: $[M L T^{-2}]$

Work-Energy Theorem

Lec 2

Identity stating total net work done by all acting forces equals the change in kinetic energy: $W_{total} = \Delta K$.

SI Unit: J (Joule) Dim: $[M L^2 T^{-2}]$

Conservative Force

Lec 3

A force whose work is path-independent, depends only on endpoints, and equals zero over closed loops ($\oint \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{s} = 0$).

Property: $W_{cons} = -\Delta U$ Ex: Gravity, Springs

Torque ($\tau$)

Lec 4

Quantitative measure of a force's capability to alter rotational motion about a pivot axis: $\tau = r F \sin\phi$.

SI Unit: N·m Dim: $[M L^2 T^{-2}]$

Moment of Inertia ($I$)

Lec 4

Rotational inertia representing resistance to changes in angular speed: $I = \sum m_i r_i^2 = \int r^2 dm$.

SI Unit: kg·m² Dim: $[M L^2]$

Sheets 1 to 4 Problem-Solving Methodologies

Step-by-step analytical algorithms for solving exercises in Sheets 1, 2, 3, and 4.

S1

Sheet 1: Dimensional Analysis & Calculus Motion

  • Dimensional Homogeneity Check: Substitute physical dimensions $[M, L, T]$. Ensure constant coefficients like $2\pi$ are treated as dimensionless $[1]$.
  • Calculus Turning Points: Given $x(t)$, differentiate once to get $v(t)$. Set $v(t) = 0$ to find the exact stopping time $t_{stop}$, then plug back into $x(t)$.
S2

Sheet 2: Incline Friction & Spring Work

  • Free-Body Diagram (FBD) on Incline: Perpendicular force balance yields $N = mg \cos\theta$. Friction force is $f_k = \mu_k mg \cos\theta$.
  • Spring Compression Stopping Distance: Apply Work-Energy Theorem $W_{net} = \Delta K \implies -\frac{1}{2}kx^2 - f_k x = -\frac{1}{2}mv_i^2$.
S3

Sheet 3: Energy Conservation & Roller Coasters

  • Pendulum Height Geometry: Height at angle $\theta$ relative to bottom point is $h = L(1 - \cos\theta)$. Equate $mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$.
  • Thermal Dissipation: Total thermal energy generated by non-conservative friction is $\Delta U_{int} = f_k d = -\Delta E_{mech}$.
S4

Sheet 4: Rotational Dynamics & Mass-Pulley Systems

  • Coupled Linear-Rotational Systems: For falling mass $m$ attached to disk pulley $I$: Linear FBD $mg - T = ma$. Rotational FBD $T R = I \alpha$. Constraint $a = R \alpha \implies a = \frac{mg}{m + I/R^2}$.
  • Compound Moment of Inertia: $I_{total} = I_{rim} + N \cdot I_{spokes} = M_{rim} R^2 + N (\frac{1}{3} m_{spoke} L^2)$.
